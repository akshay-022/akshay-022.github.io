Measuring people right will likely fix education

If we fix how people are measured and given opportunities, the free market will apply pressure to make education better. One of the big reasons education is tough to change is because measuring people for jobs is difficult and degrees are not very correlated with competence beyond intelligence and conscientiousness.

There are startups that change the counterfactual and startups that do not

Which companies didn't change the counterfactual much? Cursor, B2B SaaS etc.

Companies that did change it : SpaceX (brought civilizations on other planets 1-3 decades forward around the singularity), Anthropic (changed the dynamics of AI development)

It's much better to build companies that change the counterfactual than not, and things which change the counterfactual are usually ones that are highly time/order-sensitive or outside of free market forces (like x-risks which cannot be priced in because we have no priors to ground on)

Ambition is often memetic desire

I believe ambition for it's own sake is bad. The ideal is that once you have enough money for a comfortable life and enough freedom, needing to be remembered or needing power or needing to self-actualize through a billion dollar company to be fulfilled is undesirable. Given physical and emotional comfort fulfilment can be achieved internally.

There is a kind of ambition that says "Given my abilities I must do the best I can to help others". But this comes from a place of "this is super important for the world" not "I want to self-actualize"

Post-AGI once humans can't contribute to productivity I expect the ambitious to be the most at unease

Democracy can be made much more rational

Today Fox News or CNN influence opinions very much, and people don't think too much for themselves. Rationally forecasting downstream changes from policy, irrespective of narratives, is very computationally expensive.

People are often rational players when they're given all the information in an easy to understand way.

Maybe we should have a personal AI that analyses each policy based on our personal incentives for us, crowdsources new ideas across people, shows us the top options with impartial takes. Democracy may work much better

BCIs will feel much more natural than one may think

When we think, thoughts and memories pop into our heads fairly involuntarily. I see a world where a planted thought feels like an organic thought.

Capitalism will fail very soon

AI will lead to too much wealth concentration. For critical thinking's sake let's think in the limit, Tesla makes all the robots, OpenAI and a couple of labs make all the AI, and six mining companies hold all the resources. Those ten companies are responsible for producing everything in the world, so all the wealth accrues to them, everyone else has none, and capitalism breaks down.

Human incentives post-capitalism

Today, money is the proxy for how valuable the things people do are. Post-AGI some things will still be scarce (status, competition for a partner), but most things won't be.

We may need alternative proxies for the value of human actions, and allocate resources and status based on it. This will likely also help with meaning and give some external motivation to push against hedonism.

This also ties into whether you measure people well, and whether you help them develop based on that measurement. This is what I want Polymath to become post-AGI.

On being artistic

A big part of taste in music or literature is being very concrete. Things usually fall flat when people think "this sounds like an obvious platitude/is generic"

Being concrete evokes very vivid imagery and that tends to stick.

I believe taste is much less ability, much more proclivity. If you have an affinity to something you try to expose yourself to more of it and get better taste. So pay attention to writing that makes you feel deeply, think deeply about other people's emotions. Think about why you love certain kinds of art

Embodying it also helps, dress better, keep pretty things in your house.

The purpose of universes

Each universe may be a sub-simulation run by another universe: a way to simulate that same universe in a coarser, faster way and make predictions about its own future. If every civilization runs simulations like this, the purpose of each one may be to find a way to survive in your own universe before the death of your stars.

On faith

Pascal had this thing called Pascal's Wager. If you believe in God and you're right, you go to heaven. If you believe in God and you're wrong, you seem like a bit of a fool. If you don't believe in God and you're right, you don't gain anything. If you don't believe in God and God is real, you go to hell. Hence, you should believe in God. That's a stupid argument. You can say that about anything: "if I don't step on this tile in the next five seconds, I'll die." The likelihood estimates in the 4*4 matrix are what matter more.

There is an alternate version grounded more in reality. Having read so many biographies, it's clear that Rockefeller, Franklin, many others drew incredible strength from God and their faith. I've read a book on the neuroscience of loneliness, and there are only two known fixes to loneliness - making friends, and finding God. That's why so many old folks turn to God when they're old, and it gives them a lot of peace.

Even just instrumentally, believing in God will greatly help you. I choose to put aside my epistemic honesty in this one sector of life because life will be so much better with faith.
