On art and life

I appreciate art deeply and it's added much colour to my life. This page has my thoughts on art, life, and my music.

What's life about for me?

I want my life to be 20% about personal fulfillment and 80% about helping the world. I'm someone who feels happy very easily. My average happiness is almost always a 9 or a 10, as long as I feel socially fulfilled and have friends around me. (Although without that, it drops pretty quick).

So I don't need to spend all of my time on my personal fulfillment, and what do I do with the extra time? Help the world. That's what brings me to the 80-20 principle for my life.

So what do I do with the 20%?

Exploring the different parts of consciousness is very important to me. That does not mean having every experience. If you have a guitar, the goal is not to learn how to play every single song in the world. That's impossible, and it will make you miserable. The goal is to play all six strings, and the greatest chords, a few times. It's the same with life.

There's a short film called The Butterfly Circus, about a man with no arms and no legs. Whole areas of experience are simply closed to him, and he could spend his whole life miserable about the ones he will never have. But the best thing he can do is go as wide as he can inside everything that is open to him, and that is the most fulfilling life there is. The purpose is to live the most enriching life you can within the constraints you were born into.

Some things I really like to do:

  • Grabbing coffee and walking in the sun
  • Spending time with a friend and understanding how they feel
  • Listening to music after drinking some Red Bull
  • Grabbing some pizza and wine and having a movie night with my roommates
  • Going out into the streets of San Francisco on a sunny day when a music festival is happening, like the Fillmore Jazz Festival or the Pride parade. Anything where you can see the city's culture
  • Playing Holi with my friends in Mumbai

Music is my favourite form of art

You can listen to music for the beats and the melodies, or you can listen to the lyrics and deeply feel them. I do much more of the second. And my favourite songs are usually associated with people: people I deeply care about, memories, parts of my life, emotions I felt for different people.

Music that combines really good music with unique/deep lyrics is my favourite form of art.

Some songs show you a part of consciousness you would never have seen otherwise. Guilty as Sin? by Taylor Swift for example, is about the feeling of being in a relationship yet thinking about someone else, feeling guilty about it, being in denial, and doing it anyway. Very few other art forms can make you feel any given corner of consciousness like this.

Some of my favourite artists:

Taylor Swift has taught me a lot of what good songwriting is: how to use metaphors, how to speak in concrete terms, how to say things without saying the thing. cardigan, Guilty as Sin?, right where you left me.

Bleachers I love because instead of writing about breakups, they write about what makes relationships beautiful. Ordinary Heaven is about how it's ordinary, and it's one of the most beautiful experiences in life to witness your partner. There's also a song where a man is repeatedly getting punched in the face, drenched in the rain, about to get hit by a car, but at the end of the day his partner is there for him back home. The saxophone adds great texture. Me Before You, you and forever.

The 1975 is just very sonically beautiful. Love It If We Made It, About You.

John Mayer brought back my passion for the electric guitar, and his was the first concert that brought tears to my eyes. Slow Dancing in a Burning Room, Gravity, Edge of Desire.

I make some music myself, covers and originals, down below.

Some movies I like

Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind is a deeply emotional one. It's about a couple who use technology to wipe the memories they made with each other.

Interstellar is the first movie that made me cry. The relationship between the father and his daughter is very human and saving the world always makes for a great story.

Ram-Leela is a very visually beautiful telling of Romeo and Juliet. You feel very steeped in the culture of India. The romantic tension is very tastefully done.

My Octopus Teacher is about a man who goes scuba diving and meets an octopus. Octopuses are typically very elusive, but they become close, and you can see the octopus trust him. Then you see the octopus's whole life cycle: emotions it goes through, how it acts when predators attack it, and finally when it reproduces and dies in a programmed way. It showed me the nature of what humans are, and how we're not too different from an octopus or any other animal.
