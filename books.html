Books I really like

Some tips

  1. The best books are supposed to pull you in. I've found it much easier to avoid books that don't pull you in, those books tend to make you give up reading for many days instead of finishing the book.
  2. Install Shortform when you get started. Instead of 20 hour books it gives you 1 hour versions of the best ideas in those books and can be used in audio form. This speedruns the breadth of your knowledge.
  3. It is worth it to think about how to increase your reading speed. If you focus on it a bit, you may be able to 2x the speed at which you read blogs, highly recommend.
  4. Use AI very frequently. Ask it doubts, questions, more about the dynamics of different things, facts not in the book. My best realizations have often come from speaking to Claude about an idea the book sparked in me.
  5. Take notes as you read, ideally Obsidian which you can store locally and give Claude access to. Writing it down almost cements it in your brain. In general it's very useful to build a second brain: Building a Second Brain by Tiago Forte is a great book on this.
  6. Invest in audiobooks. It is very very important, and it will greatly increase the throughput of books you consume. You can't read while you travel or at the gym, because your eyes are needed to navigate the world. But your ears can be used for audiobooks with some practice.

Mental models

  • The Almanack of Naval Ravikant - One of my favourites, small and gives you the basics of what good judgement is.
  • Poor Charlie's Almanack - Longer but also a great book on judgement.
  • Nassim Taleb's works - Black Swan, Antifragile, Skin in the Game.
  • Ben Kuhn's blogs - Searching for outliers taught me how to look for a life partner, and how to look for the best business ideas. Also Staring into the abyss as a core life skill and Be impatient.
  • Sam Altman's blogs - especially How To Be Successful. Also Researchers and Founders, on what the best people across research and founding have in common, and What I Wish Someone Had Told Me.
  • Paul Graham - Richard Hamming's You and Your Research is a transcription of a talk by Richard Hamming on how to do Nobel-prize quality research, and Frighteningly Ambitious Startup Ideas.
  • The Beginning of Infinity - It taught me a lot of what I know about critical thinking, and a lot about epistemology: why we need progress, how we actually get it, and how the universe works. It also taught me some cool alternate quantum physics theories, and how to think about whether an argument is good or not.
  • The LessWrong sequences - this will teach you to think in likelihoods and how to critically think very well. Also great for how to not engage in wishful thinking. Reading this earlier and internalizing it would have saved me a lot of time and effort. lesswrong.com/highlights

Habits

  • Atomic Habits - Likely the book with the highest individual impact on my life. Most ways I have grown in life were built on the principles I learnt here.

Psychology & persuasion

  • How to Win Friends and Influence People
  • Influence by Robert Cialdini
  • Never Split the Difference - really cool book on persuasion and negotiating.
  • Aquarium by Viktor Suvorov - on GRU espionage: how they recruit people, how they infiltrate networks. A super interesting read into human psychology.
  • The Prince by Machiavelli, The Dictator's Handbook - Teaches you about incentives, politics, how power works.

Economics & governance

  • Lee Kuan Yew - He turned Singapore from a third world to a first world country, goes into great detail on his political journey and the decisions he made, how to be empirical in governing a country.
  • The New China Playbook - a very detailed look into exactly how China grows so fast, and how upstream policy changes affect everything downstream.
  • The Art of Strategy - a great simple game theory book. Helped with common sense intuition on personal advantages, how to think about strategy etc.
  • Inadequate Equilibria - Talks about ways the free markets are in bad Nash equilibria in the real world, what dynamics lead to things like this. If you want to have big counterfactual impact, this is a very good read.

Futurist

  • Situational Awareness by Leopold Aschenbrenner - I read this in 2024, great to learn about the AI world we will soon be in.
  • The Singularity Is Near by Ray Kurzweil - gave me a lot of really good insight into what the future holds, what tech we can expect, hard tech bets people may make.
  • Life 3.0 - It talks about humanity a thousand years in the future, a million years in the future, and a billion years in the future, and breaks down the limits of the universe using cosmology. It talks about eight different ways the future of humans and AI could go, and equally, a few bad futures it could fall into. Very thought-provoking.

Art

  • The Creative Act by Rick Rubin - One of my favourite books. It taught me how to be an artist: how to let my mind sit in an optimally fluid state, how to come up with good art, how to be in the right headspace.
  • Steve Jobs by Walter Isaacson - This did not teach me how to make better art, but inspired me to start embodying art much more. Greatly increased my affinity towards art.

Biographies

I personally find these super interesting. You get to live a thousand lives.

  • Founders podcast by David Senra - 1-2 hour long biographies of many founders, politicians, eminent people. Much much lower friction than reading 800 page biographies of each person.
  • The Mind of Napoleon - taught me a lot about institutions, policy, motivating yourself, love, courage, and the mindset it takes. Taught me how to be decisive, how much of an advantage speed gives you, good mindsets to have for success. I would highly recommend reading this.
  • Titan by Ron Chernow - on Rockefeller.
  • Can't Hurt Me by David Goggins - a very good look into how far the human body can be pushed, and how much you can trust it to take. I realised my own limits were much, much higher than I thought they were.
  • The Man from the Future - on von Neumann, he's considered by many to be the smartest human of all time. I also read a neuroscience book he wrote, The Computer and the Brain, it showed me how a genius from the past thought about AI and how it compares to the way the world went today.
  • The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin - incredible inventor and politician, played a big role in America's independence. I really like this because he is one of the very few leaders who was kind and not an asshole to people.
  • Elon Musk by Walter Isaacson - a must if you want to learn how to build a startup that changes the world.
  • The Optimist (on Sam Altman) - shows how you get the world's smartest people together and how the AI revolution began.

Life

  • Bhagavad Gita - this helped me come up with the operating principle for my life, and it may really help you understand what life is all about. It teaches you how to separate the actions you do from your ego, how to dissolve your sense of self, and how to know that you are one with the universe. It shows you how you are a conduit through which you can do service towards the universe. It has given me a lot of peace.
  • Ashtavakra Gita - talks about the stages you will go through to get enlightened. I expect the last 20 years of my life to be spent meditating and reaching enlightenment this way if we crack longevity.

Some additional books I'd recommend

  • The Art of War - the first book I read on war strategy. It showed me how much inherent advantages matter when you're trying to achieve something, and that you can't just declare you're better than everyone else and expect to win where they failed. There have to be structural reasons why, because most humans have the same capability in a lot of ways: the conditions have to actually be different, or you have to deliberately make them different. It also showed me how to think rationally in high stakes situations, and that your common sense and your gut are worth trusting there.
  • Traction - for go-to-market. It walks through every distribution channel and how to be methodical about finding the one that works best for you. It made me feel like I'd closed the loop on being a founder: I knew how to build product, and now I at least know the lay of the land on distribution.
  • Founding Sales - closed the loop on B2B sales for me.
  • Dealers of Lightning - a very, very good read. It shows you the kind of culture you want inside a company for incredible innovation.
  • The Everything Store - a very interesting look into how Jeff Bezos and Amazon work. One of the biggest companies in the world, so it's cool to see the inside of it.
  • Superforecasting - how to make very accurate predictions about the future, and how to update your priors.
  • Sapiens by Yuval Noah Harari
  • A Brief History of Time - a very interesting read. Taught me a lot about how the universe works, cosmology and physics.

Other sources of information

I was loosely inspired by a Marc Andreessen tweet to structure my information intake this way:

  • Twitter in the morning - I scroll Twitter every morning. Incredible for the latest things happening in the world.
  • Podcasts - podcasts are the best way to get the latest domain expertise across every field in today's world, and to see the trends of where things are going. Brad Jacobs built 8 unicorns, and his big skill was staying up to date on all the latest trends: he copiously read the journals, newspapers and publications of his day, caught trends before anyone else and exploited them.
  • Speaking to LLMs - every time I want to pull the thread on an idea in my head I talk to Claude. This has been a big chunk of my most recent learning.
  • Deep research reports - For example, I didn't want to read a whole book on each of them, so I had ChatGPT compile the best campaigns of Caesar, Alexander and Frederick the Great.
